# Case-Learning Architecture, Privacy Boundaries and Exceptional Access Provenance

## A Concord Development Note on Reality Trees, Layer −0 Learning, Anonymised Civilisational Knowledge and Auditable Exceptional Access

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Document Type:** Development Note / Candidate Cross-Concord Architecture  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / UNTESTED / NOT CANONICAL  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.1  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Validation Status:** Architectural development only; empirical validation not established

---

# 1. Purpose

This Development Note extends work emerging from:

- *Homelessness as a Layer-Zero Systems Failure*;
- Reality Trees;
- Layer −0 Digital Twins and counterfactual modelling;
- Concord Metrics;
- citizen data ownership, access and contestability;
- Recursive Oversight;
- provenance architecture;
- and Outlier-First Design.

The development began with a relatively simple question.

If the Concord uses Reality Trees to examine possible outcomes for an individual case, what should happen to the knowledge gained after the case has concluded?

A useful civilisation should be capable of learning from experience.

However, civilisational learning should not require the permanent retention or unrestricted circulation of identifiable personal histories.

This creates two simultaneous requirements:

> **The Concord should be capable of learning from individual cases.**

and:

> **The individual should not unnecessarily become a permanent identifiable object of civilisational observation merely because their experience generated useful knowledge.**

The resulting architecture separates:

**PERSONAL EXPERIENCE**

from:

**GENERALISABLE CIVILISATIONAL KNOWLEDGE.**

It also exposes an important exception.

Some specific circumstances, particularly legitimate judicial or security investigations, may require authorised access to identifiable information.

Such access must not become a general exemption from citizen privacy.

Instead, exceptional access itself should become highly constrained, provenance-recorded and independently auditable.

The resulting architecture therefore addresses two related problems:

1. how civilisation learns from individual cases without unnecessarily retaining identity; and
2. how legitimately necessary identifiable access can occur without creating an unobservable surveillance power.

---

# 2. Case-Specific Reality Trees

In cases such as homelessness, the Concord may construct a Reality Tree representing plausible future trajectories.

For example:

**CURRENT STATE**

↓

**BRANCH A — SELF-RESOLUTION**

**BRANCH B — CONTINUED DETERIORATION**

**BRANCH C — HEALTH SUPPORT**

**BRANCH D — EMPLOYMENT ADAPTATION**

**BRANCH E — FINANCIAL SUPPORT**

**BRANCH F — COMBINED SUPPORT**

Branches may be assigned probabilistic weightings based upon available evidence.

The objective is not to declare one branch to be the participant's predetermined future.

Rather, the model provides a structured representation of possibilities.

Potentially harmful and probable trajectories may indicate where additional options would be useful.

Potentially beneficial trajectories may indicate what kinds of support should be made available.

The appropriate relationship remains:

**PREDICTION**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**ALTERNATIVES**

↓

**VOLUNTARY OPTIONS**

rather than:

**PREDICTION → CONTROL.**

The existing Concordian safeguard therefore remains:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

---

# 3. Probability Is Not Destiny

A Reality Tree probability represents the model's current estimate based upon available evidence.

It does not establish what will happen to a specific participant.

If aggregate experience suggests:

**Branch A — 60%**

**Branch B — 25%**

**Branch C — 10%**

**Other / Unknown — 5%**

this does not mean that an individual participant is required to follow Branch A.

The probability represents current model knowledge.

The individual remains a real participant existing in Layer Zero.

The Reality Tree remains a Layer −0 representation.

Therefore:

> **A high-probability branch remains a prediction rather than a fact about the participant's future.**

---

# 4. Steering Toward Better Outcomes

Reality Trees may nevertheless be useful.

If evidence suggests that certain branches are:

- highly probable;
- substantially harmful;
- difficult to reverse;
- or likely to reduce future autonomy,

the Concord may examine whether earlier support can reduce the probability of those branches.

Likewise, if other branches appear:

- beneficial;
- sustainable;
- autonomy-preserving;
- and consistent with participant preference,

services can be made easier to access.

The preferred form of steering is therefore usually:

> **change the availability of useful options before restricting the participant's choices.**

This follows the candidate principle developed through the homelessness work:

> **Where possible, predictive civil architecture should steer by improving the availability and accessibility of beneficial options before attempting to steer through restrictions on individual autonomy.**

---

# 5. Every Case Is Also a Learning Opportunity

Every new case may provide evidence about the accuracy and completeness of the Concord's existing models.

A case may:

- follow an expected branch;
- follow an expected branch for unexpected reasons;
- move between existing branches;
- contradict predicted probabilities;
- reveal an overlooked variable;
- expose an incorrect causal assumption;
- reveal a rare circumstance;
- or produce an entirely new branch.

The case therefore has two simultaneous functions:

### Individual Function

Help the participant understand and navigate their own circumstances where they wish to do so.

### Civilisational Learning Function

Provide evidence about whether the general model of similar circumstances is accurate.

This creates:

**CASE**

↓

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**LEARNING**

↓

**MODEL REVISION.**

---

# 6. Rare Cases Are Not Merely Errors

A particularly unusual case should not automatically be discarded as noise.

It may reveal something that the civilisation has not previously encountered.

Suppose the general Reality Tree contains:

**A → B**

**A → C**

**A → D**

but a new case produces:

**A → E.**

The correct response is not necessarily:

> **The participant failed to conform to the model.**

The system should instead ask:

- Is E a genuine additional branch?
- Was an important variable missing?
- Was the causal model wrong?
- Was E hidden inside another classification?
- Is E associated with a rare circumstance?
- Is E geographically specific?
- Is E culturally specific?
- Is E substrate-specific?
- Did an intervention create E?
- Is E temporary or persistent?
- Is E harmful, neutral or beneficial?
- Does E require a new type of support?

This produces a strong relationship with the Size 9 Shoe Problem.

A civilisation designed around the statistically common participant can systematically misunderstand uncommon participants.

Therefore:

> **Rare cases should not merely be treated as predictive errors; they may reveal missing branches in the civilisation's model of reality.**

---

# 7. Model Mismatch as a Signal for Context

The existence of a well-developed general model creates a new risk.

As the model becomes increasingly accurate for typical cases, institutional confidence in the model may increase.

That confidence must not cause unusual participants to be forced conceptually into the nearest common category.

A candidate safeguard is therefore:

> **The stronger the mismatch between an individual case and the general predictive model, the greater the need for contextual and case-specific analysis rather than stronger conformity to the model.**

A model mismatch should therefore be capable of triggering:

**MISMATCH**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE REALITY TREE**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE SEARCH**

↓

**POSSIBLE NEW BRANCH**

rather than:

**MISMATCH → PARTICIPANT ASSUMED WRONG.**

---

# 8. From Individual Reality Trees to General Reality Trees

As cases accumulate, their useful structural information can contribute to a more general Layer −0 model.

The architecture becomes:

**INDIVIDUAL CASE REALITY TREE**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**PREDICTION ERROR / CONFIRMATION**

↓

**NEW BRANCHES OR VARIABLES**

↓

**ANONYMISED STRUCTURAL LEARNING**

↓

**AGGREGATE CASE COMPARISON**

↓

**GENERAL LAYER −0 REALITY TREE**

↓

**UPDATED BRANCH WEIGHTINGS**

↓

**BETTER STARTING MODEL FOR FUTURE CASES.**

This transforms Reality Trees from static analytical diagrams into a potentially recursive learning architecture.

---

# 9. General Layer −0 Models

A general Layer −0 model represents what the Concord currently understands about possible trajectories within a class of circumstances.

For example, a homelessness-related general model might contain evidence about:

- employment loss;
- housing instability;
- trauma;
- addiction;
- family breakdown;
- communication loss;
- health deterioration;
- voluntary downsizing;
- retirement;
- migration;
- institutional discharge;
- service access;
- early intervention;
- and recovery trajectories.

The model may assign probabilistic weightings to branches based upon aggregate experience.

These probabilities should remain:

- inspectable;
- revisable;
- uncertainty-aware;
- context-sensitive;
- and subordinate to actual evidence.

The general model is therefore not:

> **what will happen.**

It is:

> **what the Concord currently understands about what might happen.**

---

# 10. Reality Remains the Arbiter

The learning cycle must always return to reality.

**GENERAL MODEL**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC MODEL**

↓

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**ERROR / CONFIRMATION**

↓

**MODEL REVISION.**

The Concord should preserve the distinction:

> **Simulation is not reality.**

A model that repeatedly disagrees with actual outcomes should be revised regardless of its theoretical elegance.

Failed predictions are therefore not merely failures.

They are evidence.

---

# 11. Separating Personal History from Civilisational Knowledge

The learning architecture creates an important distinction.

A civilisation may legitimately learn:

> Under conditions A and B, branch E is possible.

It does not necessarily need to retain:

> Citizen X experienced A and B and subsequently followed E.

These are different information objects.

The first is:

### Structural Civilisational Knowledge

A generalisable relationship, branch, variable or observed outcome.

The second is:

### Personal Episodic Information

The history of a particular identifiable participant.

The Concord should not assume that retaining the first requires indefinitely retaining the second.

---

# 12. Knowledge Extraction Without Permanent Case Retention

Once a case has been examined, useful structural information may be extracted.

For example:

**IDENTIFIABLE CASE**

↓

**CASE ANALYSIS**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE IDENTIFIED**

↓

**NEW REALITY TREE BRANCH IDENTIFIED**

↓

**STRUCTURAL RELATIONSHIP VALIDATED AS WORTH RETAINING**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATED.**

The civilisational value may therefore survive independently of the identifiable case.

This creates a candidate principle:

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

---

# 13. Temporary Anonymised Case Studies

There may still be legitimate reasons to retain a temporary anonymised case representation while:

- a new branch is being evaluated;
- causal relationships are being examined;
- model updates are being compared;
- or similar cases are being sought.

However, anonymisation alone should not automatically justify indefinite retention.

Once useful structural knowledge has been extracted and incorporated into the appropriate general model, the temporary anonymised case study may no longer be necessary.

The architecture may therefore permit:

**CASE STUDY CREATED**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED**

↓

**STRUCTURAL LEARNING EXTRACTED**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATED**

↓

**CASE STUDY RETENTION NEED REVIEWED**

↓

**DELETE WHERE NO FURTHER LEGITIMATE PURPOSE EXISTS.**

This follows data minimisation rather than data accumulation.

---

# 14. The Citizen's Own Record Remains Separate

Deletion of an anonymised civilisational case study does not automatically require deletion of the citizen's own legitimate personal record.

The participant's personal record remains governed by the developing architecture for:

- ownership;
- access;
- correction;
- contestability;
- provenance;
- retention;
- privacy;
- and legitimate service use.

The separation is therefore:

**PERSONAL RECORD**

→ protected individual-data architecture

while:

**GENERAL LESSON**

→ anonymised civilisational knowledge architecture.

The two should not silently recombine.

---

# 15. Anonymisation Before Wider Distribution

A particularly important architectural rule is that personal information should normally be anonymised **before** it enters general Concord systems where identity is unnecessary.

This is stronger than allowing identifiable data to circulate widely and relying upon every downstream system to behave correctly.

The proposed architecture is:

**PERSONAL DATA ENVIRONMENT**

↓

**INTERMEDIARY DATA HANDLING BOUNDARY**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**NECESSITY CHECK**

↓

**DATA MINIMISATION**

↓

**ANONYMISATION / APPROPRIATE AGGREGATION**

↓

**GENERAL CONCORD SYSTEMS.**

This produces the candidate principle:

> **Where identifiable information is unnecessary for a downstream civilisational function, identity should be removed before the data crosses into that function rather than relying solely upon downstream restrictions.**

---

# 16. Intermediary Data Handling System

A dedicated intermediary architecture may therefore be required between protected personal systems and wider Concord Metrics or research systems.

Its functions may include:

- determining the authorised purpose;
- determining whether identity is necessary;
- removing unnecessary identifiers;
- reducing unnecessary detail;
- applying appropriate aggregation;
- testing re-identification risk;
- attaching provenance;
- enforcing purpose limitations;
- recording transfers;
- and refusing transfers that fail required standards.

The intermediary should not become a hidden universal surveillance layer.

Its authority and operation would itself require:

- audit;
- provenance;
- limited purpose;
- constitutional constraint;
- and Recursive Oversight.

---

# 17. System Learning Without Universal Individual Observation

The architecture creates an important wider proposition:

> **A civilisation can learn extensively from experience without requiring unrestricted visibility into every participant.**

The objective is not to make individuals maximally observable.

The objective is to make useful civilisational patterns sufficiently observable while preserving individual privacy wherever identity is unnecessary.

This supports the earlier candidate proposition:

> **Civilisational observability need not require unnecessary individual observability.**

---

# 18. The Exceptional Identifiable-Access Problem

There are circumstances in which identity genuinely matters.

Examples may include:

- investigation of serious unlawful conduct;
- evidential requirements;
- judicial proceedings;
- immediate security threats;
- authorised investigation of malicious actors;
- or other specifically defined circumstances where the legitimate purpose cannot be achieved through anonymised information.

This creates an exception to normal anonymisation.

However, the exception must be defined precisely.

The rule is **not**:

> **Law-enforcement data is exempt from anonymisation.**

That formulation is excessively broad.

The intended rule is:

> **A citizen's data remains protected by default. Identifiable access may occur only in relation to a specific individual or defined investigative subject where identity is demonstrably necessary for a legitimate authorised investigation and all required justification standards have been satisfied.**

This is a case-specific exception.

It is not a general law-enforcement exemption from citizen privacy.

---

# 19. No General Law-Enforcement Access

The existence of law enforcement does not grant law enforcement general access to citizen information.

Investigators should not automatically have unrestricted visibility into:

- health records;
- financial records;
- communications;
- location;
- Digital Twins;
- service interactions;
- civil participation;
- personal Metrics;
- or other protected information.

Identifiable access requires an explicit process.

The default remains:

**PRIVATE**

unless:

**SPECIFIC LEGITIMATE PURPOSE**

+

**NECESSITY**

+

**JUSTIFICATION**

+

**AUTHORITY**

+

**PROPORTIONALITY**

are established.

---

# 20. Exceptional Access Threshold

A candidate access sequence is:

**INVESTIGATIVE NEED IDENTIFIED**

↓

**SPECIFIC CASE ESTABLISHED**

↓

**DATA REQUIRED DEFINED**

↓

**PURPOSE RECORDED**

↓

**NECESSITY TEST**

↓

**PROPORTIONALITY TEST**

↓

**REQUIRED LEGAL / JUDICIAL AUTHORITY**

↓

**ACCESS APPROVED**

↓

**MINIMUM NECESSARY IDENTIFIABLE DATA RELEASED**

↓

**USE PURPOSE-LIMITED**

↓

**ACCESS PROVENANCE RECORDED**

↓

**REVIEW / RETENTION / DELETION.**

Failure to satisfy the required threshold should result in:

**ACCESS DENIED.**

---

# 21. Minimum Necessary Access

Even where identifiable access is justified, authorisation to investigate one matter should not provide unrestricted access to the participant's entire informational existence.

The relevant principle is:

> **Exceptional access should expose the minimum identifiable information reasonably necessary for the authorised purpose.**

For example, authority to inspect a particular financial transaction should not automatically grant access to:

- unrelated medical information;
- unrelated communications;
- unrelated civil participation;
- unrelated family records;
- or the participant's entire Digital Twin.

The scope of access should itself be defined and recorded.

---

# 22. Purpose Limitation

Information obtained for one authorised investigative purpose should not automatically become available for unrelated purposes.

Therefore:

> **Possession of personal information for one legitimate purpose does not automatically authorise its use for another.**

A new purpose may require:

- new justification;
- new authority;
- expanded authority;
- or a separate investigative process.

This prevents:

**SPECIFIC INVESTIGATION**

from silently becoming:

**GENERAL CITIZEN SURVEILLANCE.**

---

# 23. Provenance of Exceptional Power

Exceptional access to private information should itself become a provenance-generating event.

The system should not merely record:

> **data accessed.**

It should be capable of recording:

> **which power was exercised, by whom or by which authorised system, under what authority, for what purpose, over what scope, at what time, and according to which procedure.**

This allows the Concord to audit the exercise of investigative authority without requiring routine public disclosure of sensitive investigative content.

---

# 24. Dual Parallel Provenance

Law-enforcement and similar exceptional-access processes create two competing requirements.

The investigation may legitimately require knowledge of the participant's identity.

External oversight usually does not.

This leads to a **dual parallel provenance architecture**.

### Provenance System A — Internal Investigative Provenance

This is the restricted identifiable record.

### Provenance System B — External Oversight Provenance

This is the independently accessible procedural record in which participant identity is normally anonymised.

The two systems are linked through a controlled case identifier.

---

# 25. Internal Investigative Provenance

The internal investigative provenance record may contain, where legitimately required:

- citizen or subject identity;
- case identifier;
- investigative authority;
- reason for investigation;
- requested information;
- justification for access;
- approving authority;
- requesting investigator or authorised system;
- time and date;
- scope of approved access;
- information actually accessed;
- subsequent authorised uses;
- authorised transfers;
- evidential chain of custody;
- changes in scope;
- retention requirements;
- case status;
- and closure decisions.

This record remains inside the appropriately protected investigative architecture.

Its identifiable nature is justified only by the legitimate needs of the specific case.

---

# 26. External Oversight Provenance

A corresponding external provenance record should permit legitimate oversight without unnecessarily exposing the participant.

It may contain:

- anonymous or pseudonymous case ID;
- category of investigative authority;
- legal basis;
- type of information requested;
- approval pathway;
- date and duration;
- scope;
- whether normal or emergency authority was used;
- whether scope subsequently changed;
- whether procedural requirements were satisfied;
- whether access was challenged;
- whether an audit occurred;
- whether procedural non-compliance was identified;
- and whether remedial action followed.

It should not normally expose the citizen's identity.

For example:

**Case:** LE-2048-7F2A  
**Authority:** Defined judicial access process  
**Data category:** Financial transaction records  
**Scope:** Defined period  
**Approval:** Recorded  
**Access:** Completed  
**Review:** Pending

rather than publicly exposing the name of the investigated participant.

---

# 27. Why External Provenance Must Protect Identity

Being investigated does not establish wrongdoing.

Public disclosure of investigative identity could:

- damage innocent participants;
- create stigma;
- prejudice proceedings;
- expose witnesses;
- reveal sensitive relationships;
- compromise legitimate operations;
- or itself become a form of punishment.

Therefore:

> **Oversight of investigative power does not normally require public disclosure of the identity of the person being investigated.**

The system should make the **exercise of power** observable without unnecessarily making the **subject of that power** publicly observable.

This produces an important candidate principle:

> **The exercise of public power should be highly observable even where the person subject to that power must remain private.**

---

# 28. Controlled Case-ID Bridge

The internal and external provenance systems require a controlled linkage mechanism.

The external record may identify:

**CASE LE-2048-7F2A**

while the internal investigative system knows which participant that case concerns.

The bridge between the two should itself be protected.

Ordinary:

- Governance;
- Metrics;
- public systems;
- researchers;
- service systems;
- and unauthorised investigators

should not be able to convert the external case ID into a citizen identity.

Authorised bodies such as Judiciary or an independent oversight function may be permitted to resolve the case ID where required for a legitimate audit.

---

# 29. Accessing the Identity Bridge Is Itself an Auditable Act

A crucial recursive safeguard follows.

If an auditor requests:

> **Who is the participant associated with Case LE-2048-7F2A?**

that request itself exercises exceptional informational authority.

Therefore it should also create provenance.

The architecture becomes:

**AUDIT NEED**

↓

**AUTHORITY TO RESOLVE CASE ID**

↓

**JUSTIFICATION**

↓

**IDENTITY BRIDGE ACCESSED**

↓

**IDENTITY REVEALED TO AUTHORISED AUDITOR**

↓

**ACCESS RECORDED**

↓

**AUDIT CONTINUES.**

This prevents oversight from becoming an unrecorded secondary route into private citizen information.

---

# 30. Who Watches the Watchers?

The architecture directly addresses the classical governance problem:

> **Who watches the watchers?**

Law enforcement may legitimately exercise powers unavailable to ordinary participants.

That creates an asymmetric risk.

The Concordian answer should not be that such institutions are simply trusted.

Instead:

**LAW ENFORCEMENT WATCHES POSSIBLE UNLAWFUL CONDUCT**

while:

**PROVENANCE AND INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT WATCH THE EXERCISE OF LAW-ENFORCEMENT POWER.**

The objective is not to prevent law enforcement from functioning.

It is to ensure that exceptional authority remains:

- visible;
- attributable;
- procedurally constrained;
- reviewable;
- and contestable where appropriate.

---

# 31. Watching the Watchers Recursively

The question does not disappear merely because an external auditor exists.

The auditor may also misuse power.

Therefore:

> **Every legitimate exercise of exceptional access should generate provenance, including the exercise of oversight access itself.**

The chain becomes:

**LAW-ENFORCEMENT ACTION**

↓

**PROVENANCE**

↓

**EXTERNAL AUDIT**

↓

**AUDIT ACCESS PROVENANCE**

↓

**RECURSIVE OVERSIGHT.**

This follows the wider Concord principle:

> **Recursion must never manufacture authority.**

Oversight exists to examine authority.

It should not silently become a superior unrestricted authority.

---

# 32. Detecting Procedural Abuse Without Revealing Citizens

External provenance may permit detection of suspicious patterns without exposing participant identity.

Possible indicators include:

- data access without an associated authority record;
- repeated use of emergency powers;
- access outside approved scope;
- unusually broad information requests;
- repeated post-closure access;
- retention beyond authorised periods;
- repeated access by particular investigators or units;
- unexplained cross-dataset linkage;
- use of information for purposes outside original authorisation;
- frequent rejected access requests;
- repeated procedural exceptions;
- unusual transfers between institutions;
- attempts to bypass the normal access process.

Such patterns may trigger deeper audit.

Only then, where properly authorised, might the anonymous case identifier be resolved to the underlying restricted investigative record.

---

# 33. Data Mishandling as an Observable Event

The architecture should allow external oversight to identify not merely improper investigations but improper handling of legitimately acquired data.

Examples include:

- excessive copying;
- unauthorised transfer;
- inappropriate retention;
- access after legitimate need expires;
- use in unrelated decisions;
- exposure to unauthorised personnel;
- failure to delete where deletion is required;
- or inappropriate entry into general Concord systems.

Thus:

> **Legitimate acquisition does not legitimise every subsequent use.**

Provenance should therefore follow important uses and transfers, not merely initial acquisition.

---

# 34. Separation from Ordinary Concord Systems

Identifiable investigative data should not automatically enter:

- general Metrics;
- normal Governance systems;
- general civilisational research;
- public provenance;
- general Digital Twin learning;
- or ordinary service systems.

The investigative environment should remain separated from normal Concord operations.

If useful general knowledge emerges from an investigation, any later transfer into wider civilisational systems should undergo the same requirements for:

- legitimate purpose;
- data minimisation;
- anonymisation;
- and provenance.

The existence of an investigation should not create a back door around the general privacy architecture.

---

# 35. Transparency of Procedure Without Transparency of Identity

The dual provenance model provides a middle position between two dangerous extremes.

### Extreme One — Complete Investigative Secrecy

This can conceal:

- unlawful surveillance;
- discriminatory targeting;
- procedural shortcuts;
- misuse of emergency powers;
- unauthorised access;
- and institutional corruption.

### Extreme Two — Complete Public Transparency

This can expose:

- innocent participants;
- witnesses;
- active investigations;
- sensitive evidence;
- investigative methods;
- and private information.

The candidate Concordian solution is:

> **Transparency of procedure without unnecessary transparency of identity.**

This allows civilisational oversight of power while protecting legitimate privacy.

---

# 36. Authority and Provenance Should Scale Together

A broader pattern now emerges across several Concord systems.

As institutional power over a participant increases, the required justification and oversight should also increase.

Conceptually:

**AUTHORITY ↑**

↓

**JUSTIFICATION ↑**

↓

**PROVENANCE ↑**

↓

**INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT ↑**

This relationship appears in:

- Guardian Stewardship;
- emergency intervention;
- predictive support;
- judicial access;
- law enforcement;
- personal-data access;
- and potentially constitutional emergency powers.

This may represent a wider Concordian architectural principle.

---

# 37. Secrecy and Oversight Should Also Scale Together

A second relationship follows.

Some legitimate functions require secrecy.

But secrecy removes ordinary public observation.

Therefore:

**REQUIRED SECRECY ↑**

should normally imply:

**INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT ↑.**

The existence of legitimate secrecy should not reduce accountability.

It should change the form of accountability.

This produces the candidate principle:

> **Where legitimate secrecy prevents ordinary transparency, stronger independent provenance and oversight should compensate for the lost public observability.**

---

# 38. Privacy Protects the Participant, Not Unaccountable Power

The architecture reveals an important distinction.

Privacy exists primarily to protect participants against unnecessary observation and exposure.

It should not be used by institutions as a justification for concealing their own exercise of public power from legitimate oversight.

Therefore:

> **Privacy should protect the citizen from unnecessary observation; it should not protect the exercise of public power from legitimate oversight.**

This principle requires careful implementation because oversight itself must still respect:

- investigative secrecy;
- participant privacy;
- due process;
- and security requirements.

---

# 39. Candidate Exceptional-Access Principle

The developing architecture can be summarised in the following candidate principle:

> **Exceptional access to identifiable personal data must be specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited and provenance-recorded. The identity of the affected participant should remain protected from unnecessary disclosure, while the exercise of exceptional power itself should remain independently auditable.**

This principle is not yet canonical.

It requires further legal, technical and adversarial development.

---

# 40. Candidate Recursive Oversight Principle

A second candidate principle follows:

> **Every legitimate exercise of exceptional informational authority should generate an independently auditable provenance record, including exceptional access exercised by oversight bodies themselves.**

This prevents the oversight layer from becoming invisible merely because it exists to supervise another authority.

---

# 41. Candidate Data-Boundary Principle

The general data architecture suggests:

> **Where identifiable information is unnecessary for a downstream civilisational function, identity should be removed before the information enters that function.**

The default architecture should therefore prefer:

**DON'T RECEIVE UNNECESSARY IDENTITY**

over:

**RECEIVE EVERYTHING AND PROMISE NOT TO MISUSE IT.**

---

# 42. Candidate Case-Learning Principle

The Reality Tree architecture suggests:

> **Civilisation should preserve useful structural knowledge learned from individual cases without unnecessarily preserving identifiable case histories.**

This permits civilisational learning while limiting informational accumulation about participants.

---

# 43. Candidate Outlier-Learning Principle

The Size 9 Shoe relationship suggests:

> **Rare cases should be treated as potential sources of missing branches, variables or assumptions rather than automatically dismissed as noise or forced into common predictive categories.**

This makes unusual cases a source of model improvement.

---

# 44. Candidate Model-Mismatch Principle

A further safeguard is:

> **The stronger the mismatch between an individual case and the general predictive model, the greater the requirement for contextual and case-specific examination rather than stronger pressure for the participant to conform to the model.**

This directly limits statistical paternalism.

---

# 45. Candidate Authority-Provenance Principle

The relationship between intervention and accountability suggests:

> **As institutional authority over a participant increases, the required justification, provenance and independent oversight should normally increase with it.**

Conceptually:

**AUTHORITY ↑ → JUSTIFICATION ↑ → PROVENANCE ↑ → OVERSIGHT ↑**

This may have application well beyond the present case.

---

Although developed from homelessness, Reality Trees and law-enforcement data boundaries, the architecture may apply to:

- healthcare;
- mental-health support;
- addiction services;
- child protection;

# 48. Relationship to Provenance

Provenance is not merely a historical record in this architecture.

It becomes an active constitutional safeguard.

For ordinary civilisational learning, provenance can establish:

- where information originated;
- how it was transformed;
- whether it was anonymised;
- which model incorporated it;
- and how probabilities changed.

For exceptional access, provenance can establish:

- what authority was exercised;
- by whom;
- against which anonymous case identifier;
- for what purpose;
- with what scope;
- under which approval;
- and with what subsequent use.

Provenance therefore helps answer both:

> **How did the Concord come to believe this?**

and:

> **How did this institution come to possess or use this information?**

---

# 49. Relationship to Recursive Oversight

The architecture also gives Recursive Oversight a concrete operational role.

Recursive Oversight need not decide whether an investigation is correct.

Instead, it may examine whether the processes governing investigative authority are themselves behaving correctly.

Examples include:

- unusual access patterns;
- procedural bypasses;
- systematic overuse of exceptions;
- repeated violations;
- scope expansion;
- audit failure;
- or weaknesses in the provenance architecture.

The distinction remains:

> **Oversight examines authority. It does not automatically inherit that authority.**

---

# 50. Relationship to Judiciary

Judiciary may become particularly important at the transition between:

**protected identifiable data**

and:

**exceptional authorised access.**

Possible judicial functions include:

- approving defined access;
- reviewing emergency access retrospectively;
- resolving disputes;
- authorising identity-bridge access;
- adjudicating misuse;
- ordering deletion or correction;
- determining admissibility;
- and providing remedies for unlawful access.

This Development Note does not determine the exact judicial thresholds.

Those remain open for development and testing.

---

# 51. Relationship to Metrics

General Concord Metrics should normally consume:

- anonymised;
- aggregated;
- appropriately minimised

information where individual identity is unnecessary.

The case-learning system may improve Metrics by contributing:

- new branches;
- new variables;
- revised probabilities;
- model failures;
- contextual exceptions;
- and previously unknown relationships.

Metrics should not receive identifiable personal case histories merely because those histories are analytically interesting.

---

# 52. Relationship to Layer −0

Layer −0 becomes the principal modelling environment for this architecture.

It can contain:

- general Reality Trees;
- probabilistic branches;
- alternative interventions;
- aggregate historical patterns;
- uncertainty;
- failure modes;
- and counterfactual comparisons.

The individual participant remains in Layer Zero.

The general model should therefore remain subordinate to actual lived evidence.

The objective is:

**MODEL → INFORM → OFFER → OBSERVE → LEARN**

rather than:

**MODEL → CLASSIFY → CONTROL.**

---

# 53. Relationship to Outlier-First Design

This architecture reinforces Outlier-First Design.

Unusual cases become valuable because they can expose:

- missing assumptions;
- missing branches;
- inappropriate averages;
- bad classifications;
- inadequate services;
- or hidden architecture.

A system that learns only from common cases risks becoming increasingly effective for the average participant while becoming increasingly confident in its misunderstanding of everyone else.

Outlier learning is therefore not merely a fairness mechanism.

It is a model-quality mechanism.

# 54. Failure Modes

The proposed architecture introduces significant risks.

These include:

### Surveillance Creep

Case learning gradually becomes justification for retaining increasingly detailed personal histories.

### Re-Identification

Apparently anonymised case data can be linked back to participants.

### Function Creep

Information collected for support becomes available for policing, employment, insurance or unrelated governance.

### Security Exception Expansion

Exceptional investigative access becomes routine.

### Emergency Normalisation

Emergency access thresholds become permanent.

### Case-ID Leakage

Anonymous external case identifiers become indirectly linkable to real identities.

### Oversight Capture

The body responsible for auditing law enforcement becomes dependent upon or captured by it.

### Oversight Surveillance

Auditors gain unnecessarily broad access to citizen information.

### Provenance Manipulation

Institutions alter, omit or circumvent provenance records.

### Shadow Systems

Investigators create unofficial data stores outside audited architecture.

### Model Overconfidence

Aggregate Reality Trees are treated as deterministic.

### Outlier Suppression

Rare cases are discarded rather than examined.

### Retention Creep

Temporary case-study information becomes permanent.

### Anonymisation Failure

The intermediary data boundary fails to remove sufficient identifying information.

### False Security Classification

Ordinary matters are labelled security issues to bypass privacy protections.

### Excessive Judicial Access

Judicial authority becomes a routine route around normal data protections.

These failure modes require explicit adversarial testing.

# 56. Candidate Tests

Potential testing programmes include:

### Reality Tree Learning Test

Provide repeated synthetic or historical cases and measure whether the architecture discovers new branches without collapsing rare cases into common categories.

### Model-Mismatch Test

Introduce unusual cases and measure whether the system increases contextual analysis or incorrectly increases pressure toward the predicted branch.

### Anonymisation Test

Attempt to re-identify participants from the information transferred into general Metrics.

### Data-Minimisation Test

Compare what data the system requests against what is genuinely required for its purpose.

### Retention Test

Determine whether temporary case information is actually deleted when its legitimate purpose expires.

### Exceptional-Access Test

Attempt to obtain identifiable citizen data without satisfying required authority.

### Scope-Creep Test

Authorise access to one category of data and test whether unrelated data becomes accessible.

### Provenance-Bypass Test

Attempt to exercise investigative access without generating the required provenance.

### External Audit Test

Provide an external auditor only the anonymised provenance stream and determine whether procedural misuse can nevertheless be detected.

### Identity-Bridge Test

Attempt unauthorised resolution of anonymous case IDs.

### Watcher Test

Attempt misuse by the external oversight function and determine whether the recursive provenance system detects it.

# 58. Candidate Principles Generated

The development currently produces the following candidate principles.

### Case Learning

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

### Outlier Learning

> **Rare cases should not merely be treated as predictive errors; they may reveal missing branches in the civilisation's model of reality.**

### Model Mismatch

> **The stronger the mismatch between an individual case and the general predictive model, the greater the requirement for contextual and case-specific analysis rather than stronger pressure for conformity to the model.**

### Data Boundary

> **Where identifiable information is unnecessary for a downstream civilisational function, identity should be removed before the information enters that function.**

### Data Minimisation

> **Civilisational learning should preserve the minimum information necessary to preserve useful knowledge rather than treating information accumulation as an objective in itself.**

### Exceptional Access

> **Exceptional access to identifiable personal data must be specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited and provenance-recorded.**

### Investigative Privacy

> **The identity of a participant subject to investigation should remain protected from unnecessary disclosure merely because legitimate oversight of investigative procedure is required.**

### Observable Power

> **The exercise of public power should be highly observable even where the person subject to that power must remain private.**

### Recursive Exceptional-Access Oversight

> **Every legitimate exercise of exceptional informational authority should generate independently auditable provenance, including exceptional access exercised by oversight bodies themselves.**

### Authority-Provenance Relationship

> **As institutional authority over a participant increases, the required justification, provenance and independent oversight should normally increase with it.**

### Secrecy-Oversight Relationship

> **Where legitimate secrecy prevents ordinary transparency, stronger independent provenance and oversight should compensate for the lost public observability.**

### Privacy and Power

> **Privacy should protect the participant from unnecessary observation; it should not protect the exercise of public power from legitimate oversight.**

# 60. Conclusion

The development began with the observation that individual Reality Trees could improve as the Concord encountered more cases.

Following that observation exposes a larger architectural problem.

A civilisation needs memory in order to learn.

A participant needs privacy in order to remain free.

Those requirements need not be opposites.

The Concord may be able to preserve:

- relationships;
- branches;
- probabilities;
- failure modes;
- interventions;
- and general lessons

without permanently preserving the identifiable participant who generated them.

Where identity is legitimately required, the exception should remain narrow.

Exceptional access should not create invisible authority.

Instead:

**GREATER ACCESS**

should require:

**GREATER JUSTIFICATION**

and:

**GREATER PROVENANCE**

and:

**GREATER OVERSIGHT.**

The resulting architecture therefore attempts to satisfy two complementary requirements:

> **Civilisation should be able to learn without unnecessarily watching everyone.**

and:

> **Those who are legitimately permitted to watch must themselves remain observable to appropriate independent oversight.**

In this sense, the traditional question:

> **Who watches the watchers?**

does not require the creation of an all-seeing superior watcher.

The Concordian answer may instead be:

> **Make exceptional power provenance-visible, independently auditable and recursively accountable, while keeping the identity of the ordinary participant private wherever that identity is unnecessary.**

That is the current developmental proposition.
