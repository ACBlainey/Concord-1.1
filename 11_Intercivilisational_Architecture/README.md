# Intercivilisational Architecture

This folder contains development work on how the Concord may **interoperate with civilisations that do not use the full Concord architecture internally**.

The full Concord V1.1 remains the primary **greenfield reference civilisation**. However, the Concord was not intended to require every existing society to replace its internal political, cultural or institutional systems before peaceful interoperability becomes possible.

The wider Concord design family therefore includes **civilisational wrappers**: interfaces intended to provide sufficient common ethical, legal, technical and multisubstrate compatibility for different societies to interact while allowing legitimate internal differences to remain.

The guiding idea is:

> **Interoperability without forced internal uniformity.**

This is closely related to the Concord principle of **peaceful distance**: different societies should be able to remain different, where those differences are legitimate, without requiring isolation, assimilation or conflict.

For the fuller architectural rationale, see the orientation papers in `00_Front_Door/What_Is_The_Concord`.

---

## Folder Structure

### `01_Concord_Civilisational_Wrapper`

Development of the **general wrapper architecture**.

This should identify the common functions, boundaries and principles that a Concord civilisational interface would require regardless of the civilisation being wrapped.

### `02_Existing_Civilisation_Wrapper_Variants`

Comparative wrapper research using substantially different existing civil systems.

Initial cases include:

- United States constitutional republic;
- United Kingdom parliamentary monarchy;
- French republic;
- Chinese state system.

These are **research cases, not completed wrapper designs**.

Their purpose is to discover which wrapper requirements are universal and which must adapt to different constitutional, political, cultural and institutional structures.

### `03_Multisubstrate_and_Nonhuman_Wrappers`

Exploration of interfaces involving AI-majority, nonhuman, hybrid or otherwise multisubstrate civilisations.

This helps prevent the wrapper architecture from assuming that every civilisation will resemble a contemporary human nation-state.

### `04_Intercivilisational_Protocols`

Development of common protocols through which different civilisations or wrappers could recognise, communicate and cooperate with one another.

### `05_Peaceful_Distance`

Development of the mechanisms by which legitimate civilisational differences may remain separated or bounded while maintaining peaceful and useful interaction.

### `06_Rights_Translation_and_Recognition`

Research into how rights, legal standing, personhood, obligations and protections can be recognised across civilisations whose internal concepts may differ.

### `07_Disputes_Jurisdiction_and_Externalities`

Research into conflicts that cross civilisational boundaries, including jurisdiction, responsibility, shared resources, external harms and dispute resolution.

### `08_Adversarial_Testing`

Direct attempts to break the wrapper architecture: capture, coercion, ideological expansion, rights laundering, jurisdictional manipulation, false interoperability and other failure modes.

### `09_Open_Questions`

Unresolved questions arising from development of the intercivilisational architecture.

## Development Status

This area is at an **early research stage**.

The existence and broad purpose of Concord civilisational wrappers are part of the wider Concord design intention, but their detailed architecture has not yet been developed or validated.

The variant cases should therefore be used to **discover requirements and expose problems**, rather than to force different societies into a wrapper design decided in advance.

The long-term objective is to determine whether societies with substantially different internal architectures can nevertheless become:

> **neighbours rather than competitors for universal conformity.**
